These pages explain, in plain language, the conditions Dr. Katz treats and the tests and procedures you may hear about at a visit. They are meant to help you understand your care and ask good questions. Your own doctor's instructions always come first.

Glaucoma

What Is Glaucoma?

Glaucoma is damage to the optic nerve, usually linked to eye pressure. Why it has no symptoms early, who is at risk, and how it is treated.

How Treatments Lower Eye Pressure

The eye as a sink: a faucet that makes fluid, drains that carry it away, and a water level that is the pressure. See what each glaucoma treatment changes.

Glaucoma Testing

What happens at a glaucoma visit: eye pressure, the visual field test, OCT imaging, gonioscopy and corneal thickness, and what the results mean.

Using Eye Drops

Step-by-step technique for putting in eye drops, why to wait five minutes between drops, pressing the tear duct, missed doses, storage and refills.

SLT Laser

SLT: an office laser that lowers eye pressure by treating the eye's drain, through a contact lens or as direct SLT with no lens. The visit and recovery.

Laser Iridotomy

Laser iridotomy: a quick office laser that makes a tiny opening in the iris to open a narrow drainage angle. The visit, recovery and risks.

MIGS

Minimally invasive glaucoma surgery: stents, goniotomy and viscodilation, often done with cataract surgery, with a quicker recovery than older operations.

Trabeculectomy

Trabeculectomy makes a new drain under the eyelid to lower eye pressure in advanced glaucoma. The operation, the twelve-week recovery, and warning signs.

Tube Shunt Surgery

Tube shunts (Ahmed, Baerveldt): a small tube and plate that drain fluid from the eye. Who they are for, the operation, and the six-week recovery.

Micropulse and CPC Laser

Micropulse and standard CPC: a laser applied through the white of the eye that lowers pressure by reducing how much fluid the eye makes.

Cataract

Cataract Surgery

What a cataract is, when surgery makes sense, lens implant choices, the day of surgery and the four-week recovery, and what changes if you have glaucoma.

YAG Capsulotomy

YAG capsulotomy: a quick office laser that clears the cloudy capsule behind a lens implant after cataract surgery. The visit, recovery and risks.

Dry Eye

What Is Dry Eye?

Dry eye is a problem with the tear film. Why it burns, blurs and even waters, the two main kinds, what brings it on, and how it is checked.

Blepharitis and the Eyelid Glands

Blepharitis and blocked eyelid oil glands, the most common cause of dry eye: symptoms, warm compresses and lid cleaning, styes, chalazia and prescriptions.

Treating Dry Eye

What helps dry eye, step by step: habits at home, artificial tears, eyelid care, prescription drops like cyclosporine and lifitegrast, and punctal plugs.

Choosing Artificial Tears

Answer a few questions to see which artificial tear suits you: standard or preservative-free, gel drops, or ointment at bedtime, with photos.

Watery Eyes

Why eyes water: reflex tears from a dry surface, a blocked tear drain, or a loose lower lid. How the cause is found and what fixes it.

Eye Allergy

Itchy, red, watery eyes from pollen, dust or pets: how to tell allergy from dry eye and pink eye, what helps, and why allergy drops work best daily.

Cornea

Corneal Scratches

A scratched cornea: why it hurts so much, how it heals, what treatment involves, and the erosions that can come back months later.

Pterygium and Pinguecula

Pinguecula and pterygium, growths on the white of the eye from sun and wind: when they need treatment, and what surgery and recovery involve.

Fuchs Dystrophy

Fuchs dystrophy: why the cornea swells and vision is foggy on waking, what salt drops do, its effect on cataract surgery, and the transplant that treats it.

Keratoconus

Keratoconus: a cornea that thins and bulges into a cone, often from the teens. Symptoms, diagnosis, cross-linking to stop it, and lenses that restore vision.

Corneal Infections

Corneal infections and ulcers: the warning signs, contact lens risks, herpes and shingles in the eye, how they are treated, and how to prevent them.

Corneal Transplants

Corneal transplants (DMEK, DSAEK, DALK, PK): who needs one, what the operation and recovery involve, and the warning signs of rejection.

Retina

Flashes and Floaters

Why floaters and flashes appear, what happens when the gel inside the eye pulls away from the retina, and the warning signs that need a same-day exam.

Retinal Tears and Detachment

How a retinal tear leads to a detachment, the warning signs, how a tear is sealed with laser, and how a detached retina is repaired. With an animation of both.

Macular Degeneration

Dry and wet macular degeneration: what each does to vision, AREDS2 vitamins, a printable Amsler grid for home checks, and treatment with injections.

Diabetic Eye Disease

How diabetes damages the retina, why early stages have no symptoms, how often to be examined, and how retinopathy and macular swelling are treated.

Injections into the Eye

What to expect from an injection into the eye for wet macular degeneration, diabetic swelling or a vein blockage: the visit, aftercare, risks, how often.

Vitrectomy

Vitrectomy (PPV): surgery to remove the gel inside the eye to clear blood, repair a detachment or macular hole, or remove scar tissue. What recovery involves.

Laser for the Retina

Retinal laser: scatter laser (PRP) for new blood vessels in diabetic eye disease and vein blockages, and focal laser for leaks near the macula.

Vision Simulators

What Vision Loss Looks Like

An interactive simulator of glaucoma, cataract, macular degeneration, diabetic eye disease, retinal detachment, stroke, dry eye and floaters.

Your Glasses Prescription

Enter a glasses prescription and see how the world looks with and without the glasses at distance, arm's length and reading distance.
