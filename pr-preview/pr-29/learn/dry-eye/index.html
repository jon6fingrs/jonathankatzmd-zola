Learn›Dry Eye

Dry eye is one of the most common reasons eyes burn, feel gritty, water, or blur on and off. These pages explain what is going on in the tear film and the eyelids, and what can be done about it. Watery eyes and eye allergy are here too, because they overlap with dry eye and are often confused with it.

Dry eye

What Is Dry Eye?

Dry eye is a problem with the tear film. Why it burns, blurs and even waters, the two main kinds, what brings it on, and how it is checked.

Blepharitis and the Eyelid Glands

Blepharitis and blocked eyelid oil glands, the most common cause of dry eye: symptoms, warm compresses and lid cleaning, styes, chalazia and prescriptions.

Treating Dry Eye

What helps dry eye, step by step: habits at home, artificial tears, eyelid care, prescription drops like cyclosporine and lifitegrast, and punctal plugs.

Choosing Artificial Tears

Answer a few questions to see which artificial tear suits you: standard or preservative-free, gel drops, or ointment at bedtime, with photos.

Related problems

Watery Eyes

Why eyes water: reflex tears from a dry surface, a blocked tear drain, or a loose lower lid. How the cause is found and what fixes it.

Eye Allergy

Itchy, red, watery eyes from pollen, dust or pets: how to tell allergy from dry eye and pink eye, what helps, and why allergy drops work best daily.

Other topics

Glaucoma

What glaucoma is, how it is tested and followed, and what to expect from drops, laser and surgery.

Cataract

Cataract surgery from first visit to recovery, and the laser that clears a cloudy capsule afterward.

Cornea

The clear window at the front of the eye: scratches, pterygium, corneal swelling from Fuchs dystrophy, keratoconus, infections, and corneal transplants.

Retina

Flashes and floaters, retinal tears and detachment, macular degeneration, diabetic eye disease, and what to expect from injections, laser and vitrectomy.
