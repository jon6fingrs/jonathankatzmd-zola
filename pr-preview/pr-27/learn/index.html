These pages explain, in plain language, the conditions Dr. Katz treats and the tests and procedures you may hear about at a visit. They are meant to help you understand your care and ask good questions. Your own doctor's instructions always come first.

Glaucoma

What Is Glaucoma?

Glaucoma is damage to the optic nerve, usually linked to eye pressure. Why it has no symptoms early, who is at risk, and how it is treated.

How Treatments Lower Eye Pressure

The eye as a sink: a faucet that makes fluid, drains that carry it away, and a water level that is the eye pressure. Tap each glaucoma treatment (drops, SLT, MIGS, trabeculectomy, tube shunt, cyclophotocoagulation) to see what it changes.

Glaucoma Testing

What happens at a glaucoma visit: eye pressure, the visual field test, OCT imaging, gonioscopy and corneal thickness, and what the results mean.

Using Eye Drops

Step-by-step technique for putting in eye drops, why to wait five minutes between drops, pressing the tear duct, missed doses, storage and refills.

SLT Laser

Selective laser trabeculoplasty: an office laser that lowers eye pressure by treating the eye's drain, done either through a contact lens or as direct SLT (DSLT) with no lens. With a step-by-step animation, what the visit is like, and what to expect afterward.

Laser Iridotomy

Laser peripheral iridotomy: a quick office laser that makes a tiny opening in the iris to open a narrow drainage angle. With an animation of what the laser does, what the visit is like, and what to expect afterward.

MIGS

Minimally invasive glaucoma surgery: small implants and procedures, often combined with cataract surgery, that lower pressure with a quicker recovery than traditional surgery. With step-by-step animations of a stent, a goniotomy, viscodilation, and viscodilation combined with a stent.

Trabeculectomy

Trabeculectomy creates a new drainage channel under the eyelid to lower eye pressure in advanced glaucoma. With a step-by-step animation, what the operation involves, the twelve-week recovery, and warning signs.

Tube Shunt Surgery

Glaucoma drainage devices (Ahmed, Baerveldt): a small tube and plate that drain fluid from the eye. With a step-by-step animation, who they are for, the operation, and the six-week recovery.

Micropulse and CPC Laser

Cyclophotocoagulation: a laser applied to the outside of the eye that lowers pressure by reducing how much fluid the eye makes. Micropulse and standard CPC compared, with a step-by-step animation, what the day is like, and what to expect afterward.

Cataract

Cataract Surgery

What a cataract is, when surgery makes sense, lens implant choices, the day of surgery and the four-week recovery, and what is different if you also have glaucoma.

YAG Capsulotomy

YAG laser capsulotomy: a quick office laser that clears the cloudy capsule that can form behind a lens implant after cataract surgery. With an animation of what the laser does and how vision changes, what the visit is like, and what to expect afterward.

Dry Eye

What Is Dry Eye?

Dry eye is a problem with the tear film that coats the eye. Why it burns, blurs and even waters, the two main kinds, what brings it on, and how it is checked. With an animation of the tear film between blinks.

Blepharitis and the Eyelid Glands

Blepharitis is inflammation of the eyelid edges, and blocked oil glands in the lids are the most common cause of dry eye. What it looks and feels like, how to do warm compresses and lid cleaning, and when prescription treatment helps. With an animation of lid care and drawings of the lash line, styes and chalazia.

Treating Dry Eye

What helps dry eye, step by step: changes at home, artificial tears and how to choose them, eyelid care, prescription drops such as cyclosporine and lifitegrast, punctal plugs and more. With an animation of how a punctal plug works.

Choosing Artificial Tears

Answer a few questions to see which kind of artificial tear suits you: standard or preservative-free, gel drops, or ointment at bedtime, with examples and photos.

Watery Eyes

Why eyes water: reflex tears from a dry surface, a blocked tear drain, or a loose lower lid. With an animation of each, how the cause is found, and what fixes it.

Eye Allergy

Itchy, red, watery eyes from pollen, dust or pets: what an eye allergy is, how to tell it from dry eye and pink eye, what helps, and why allergy drops work best used every day. With an animation of the reaction.

Vision Simulators

What Vision Loss Looks Like

An interactive simulator of glaucoma, cataract, macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, retinal detachment, stroke, dry eye, floaters and more, with plain-language explanations of each.

Your Glasses Prescription

Enter a glasses prescription and see how the world looks with and without the glasses at distance, arm's length and reading distance.
