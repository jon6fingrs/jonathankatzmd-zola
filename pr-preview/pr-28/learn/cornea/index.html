Learn›Cornea

The cornea is the clear dome at the front of the eye. It lets light in and does most of the eye's focusing, so anything that scratches, clouds or reshapes it affects vision. These pages cover the corneal problems seen most often and how they are treated.

Conditions

Corneal Scratches

A scratched cornea (corneal abrasion): why it hurts so much, how it heals, what treatment involves, and the erosions that can come back months later. With an animation.

Pterygium and Pinguecula

Growths on the white of the eye from sun and wind: what a pinguecula and a pterygium are, when they need treatment, and what pterygium surgery and recovery involve. With an animation.

Fuchs Dystrophy

Fuchs dystrophy: why the cornea swells and vision is foggy in the morning, how it is followed, what salt drops do, how it affects cataract surgery, and the partial-thickness transplant that treats it. With an animation.

Keratoconus

Keratoconus: a cornea that thins and bulges into a cone, usually starting in the teens or twenties. Symptoms, how it is found, cross-linking to stop it, and the contact lenses that restore vision. With an animation.

Corneal Infections

Infections of the cornea (keratitis and corneal ulcers): the warning signs, why contact lens wearers are at risk, herpes and shingles in the eye, how infections are treated, and how to prevent them.

Surgery

Corneal Transplants

Corneal transplant surgery: the full-thickness and partial-thickness kinds (PK, DMEK, DSAEK), who needs one, what the operation and recovery involve, and the signs of rejection.

Other topics

Glaucoma

What glaucoma is, how it is tested and followed, and what to expect from drops, laser and surgery.

Cataract

Cataract surgery from first visit to recovery, and the laser that clears a cloudy capsule afterward.

Dry Eye

Why eyes feel dry, gritty or watery, the eyelid problems behind most dry eye, and what helps, from lid care and artificial tears to prescription drops and plugs. Also watery eyes and eye allergy.

Retina

Flashes and floaters, retinal tears and detachment, macular degeneration, diabetic eye disease, and what to expect from injections into the eye.
