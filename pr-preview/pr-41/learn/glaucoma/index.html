Learn›Glaucoma

Glaucoma is damage to the optic nerve, usually related to eye pressure. These pages explain the condition and its types, the tests used to follow it, the drops, and each laser and operation, most with an animation you can step through.

Basics

What Is Glaucoma?

Glaucoma is damage to the optic nerve, usually linked to eye pressure. Why it has no symptoms early, who is at risk, and how it is treated.

How Treatments Lower Eye Pressure

The eye as a sink: a faucet that makes fluid, drains that carry it away, and a water level that is the pressure. See what each glaucoma treatment changes.

Glaucoma Testing

What happens at a glaucoma visit: eye pressure, the visual field test, OCT imaging, gonioscopy and corneal thickness, and what the results mean.

Using Eye Drops

Step-by-step technique for putting in eye drops, why to wait five minutes between drops, pressing the tear duct, missed doses, storage and refills.

Glaucoma Eye Drops

The families of glaucoma drops: what each one does, how often it is used, its cap color and its common side effects, plus combination drops.

Types

Glaucoma Suspect and Ocular Hypertension

What it means to be called a glaucoma suspect or to have ocular hypertension: why the eye is watched, who is treated, and what the follow-up involves.

Narrow Angles and Angle Closure

What a narrow drainage angle is, the warning signs of an angle-closure attack, who is at risk, and how laser and lens surgery open the angle.

Other Types of Glaucoma

Normal-tension, pigmentary, pseudoexfoliation and secondary glaucoma, and glaucoma in children: what sets each apart and how it is treated.

Lasers

SLT Laser

SLT: an office laser that lowers eye pressure by treating the eye's drain, through a contact lens or as direct SLT with no lens. The visit and recovery.

Laser Iridotomy

Laser iridotomy: a quick office laser that makes a tiny opening in the iris to open a narrow drainage angle. The visit, recovery and risks.

Micropulse and CPC Laser

Micropulse and standard CPC: a laser applied through the white of the eye that lowers pressure by reducing how much fluid the eye makes.

Surgery

MIGS

Minimally invasive glaucoma surgery: stents, goniotomy and viscodilation, often done with cataract surgery, with a quicker recovery than older operations.

Trabeculectomy

Trabeculectomy makes a new drain under the eyelid to lower eye pressure in advanced glaucoma. The operation, the twelve-week recovery, and warning signs.

Tube Shunt Surgery

Tube shunts (Ahmed, Baerveldt): a small tube and plate that drain fluid from the eye. Who they are for, the operation, and the six-week recovery.

Other topics

Cataract

Cataract surgery from first visit to recovery, choosing a lens implant, and the laser that clears a cloudy capsule afterward.

Dry Eye

Why eyes feel dry, gritty or watery, the eyelid problems behind most dry eye, and what helps. Also watery eyes and eye allergy.

Cornea

The clear window at the front of the eye: scratches, pterygium, corneal swelling from Fuchs dystrophy, keratoconus, infections, and corneal transplants.

Retina

Flashes and floaters, retinal tears and detachment, macular degeneration, diabetic eye disease, and what to expect from injections, laser and vitrectomy.

Vision Simulators

See what eye conditions do to everyday scenes, and what a glasses prescription means, on your own screen.
