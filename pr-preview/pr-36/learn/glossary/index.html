Glossary of Eye Terms

Published Nov 13, 2026

The words below come up at eye visits and on the pages of this site. Each has a short, plain meaning, and most link to the page that explains more. The search finds any of them across the whole site.

ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPRSTUVY

A

Accommodation. The eye changing focus from far to near, by changing the shape of its lens. It fades with age (see presbyopia). More: Your Glasses Prescription

Amsler grid. A square of straight lines used at home to check each eye for new distortion or a missing patch, an early sign of change in the macula. More: Macular Degeneration

Angle (drainage angle). The corner inside the eye where the iris meets the cornea. The eye's drain, the trabecular meshwork, sits here. More: Narrow Angles and Angle Closure

Angle closure. The iris blocking the drainage angle, which can raise eye pressure slowly or suddenly. More: Narrow Angles and Angle Closure

Anti-VEGF. Medicines injected into the eye that stop abnormal blood vessels from leaking and growing, used for wet macular degeneration, diabetic eye disease and vein blockages. More: Injections into the Eye

Aqueous humor. The clear fluid that fills the front of the eye. It is made all the time and drains away, and the balance between the two sets the eye pressure. More: How Treatments Lower Eye Pressure

Astigmatism. A front of the eye curved more like a football than a basketball, so the image is smeared in one direction. Glasses, contact lenses or a toric lens implant correct it. More: Your Glasses Prescription

B

Beta blocker. A family of glaucoma drops, such as timolol, that make the eye produce less fluid. More: Glaucoma Eye Drops

Blepharitis. Inflammation of the eyelid edges, often from blocked oil glands; the most common cause of dry eye. More: Blepharitis and the Eyelid Glands

Bleb. The small blister under the upper eyelid where fluid collects and is absorbed after a trabeculectomy. More: Trabeculectomy

C

Capsule. The thin, clear bag that holds the eye's lens, and later the lens implant. When it turns cloudy after cataract surgery, a laser clears it. More: YAG Capsulotomy

Cataract. Clouding of the eye's natural lens, which blurs vision and causes glare. It is treated by replacing the lens. More: Cataract Surgery

Chalazion. A firm, usually painless lump in the eyelid from a blocked oil gland. More: Blepharitis and the Eyelid Glands

Ciliary body. The ring of tissue behind the iris that makes the eye's fluid, and holds the lens in place. More: What Is Glaucoma?

Conjunctiva. The thin, clear skin over the white of the eye and the inside of the eyelids.

Cornea. The clear dome at the front of the eye. It lets light in and does most of the eye's focusing. More: Cornea

Cross-linking. A treatment that stiffens the cornea with vitamin B2 drops and ultraviolet light, to stop keratoconus getting worse. More: Keratoconus

Cup-to-disc ratio. How much of the optic nerve's surface is taken up by its central cup. A cup that is large or growing can be a sign of glaucoma. More: Glaucoma Testing

Cyclophotocoagulation (CPC). A laser applied through the white of the eye that lowers pressure by reducing how much fluid the eye makes. More: Micropulse and CPC Laser

D

Dilation. Widening the pupil with drops so the doctor can see the lens, retina and optic nerve. Vision is blurry and bright light uncomfortable for a few hours.

Diopter. The unit of focusing power used in glasses prescriptions; minus for nearsighted, plus for farsighted. More: Your Glasses Prescription

DMEK and DSAEK. Partial-thickness corneal transplants that replace only the cornea's inner pump layer, mainly for Fuchs dystrophy. More: Corneal Transplants

Drusen. Small yellow deposits under the retina, the first sign of macular degeneration. More: Macular Degeneration

Dry eye. A problem with the tear film, from too few tears or tears that evaporate too fast. More: What Is Dry Eye?

E

Endothelium. The single layer of pump cells lining the inside of the cornea, which keeps it clear. More: Fuchs Dystrophy

Epiretinal membrane. A thin film on the surface of the macula that can wrinkle it and distort vision; also called macular pucker. More: Epiretinal Membrane

Extended depth of focus (EDOF) lens. A lens implant that gives clear vision from distance to arm's length. More: Choosing a Lens Implant

F

Farsightedness (hyperopia). An eye that focuses light behind the retina, so near vision blurs first. More: Your Glasses Prescription

Floaters. Specks, threads or cobwebs that drift across vision, from clumps in the gel inside the eye. A sudden shower of new ones needs a same-day exam. More: Flashes and Floaters

Fovea. The tiny pit at the center of the macula that gives the sharpest vision.

Fuchs dystrophy. An inherited loss of the cornea's pump cells that makes it swell and turn hazy, often worst in the morning. More: Fuchs Dystrophy

Fundus. The back of the eye as the doctor sees it: the retina, the macula, the optic nerve and the blood vessels.

G

Glaucoma. Damage to the optic nerve, usually related to eye pressure, that takes side vision first and without symptoms. More: What Is Glaucoma?

Glaucoma suspect. Someone with a finding that raises the question of glaucoma, such as high pressure or a suspicious nerve, without proof of damage. More: Glaucoma Suspect and Ocular Hypertension

Gonioscopy. An exam with a mirrored contact lens that lets the doctor see the drainage angle. More: Glaucoma Testing

H

Halos. Rings or glow around lights, especially at night. They can come from a cataract, a multifocal lens implant, a swollen cornea, or, with pain and redness, an attack of angle closure.

Hyphema. Blood in the front of the eye, after an injury or some eye surgery. It usually clears within days.

I

Intraocular lens (IOL). The clear artificial lens placed in the eye during cataract surgery. More: Choosing a Lens Implant

Intraocular pressure (IOP). The pressure inside the eye, measured in millimeters of mercury (mm Hg). More: How Treatments Lower Eye Pressure

Iridotomy. A tiny laser opening in the iris that opens a narrow drainage angle. More: Laser Iridotomy

Iris. The colored part of the eye. Its opening is the pupil.

K

Keratitis. Inflammation or infection of the cornea. More: Corneal Infections

Keratoconus. A cornea that thins and bulges into a cone, blurring and distorting vision. More: Keratoconus

L

Lens. The clear structure behind the iris that fine-tunes the eye's focus. When it clouds, it is a cataract.

M

Macula. The small central part of the retina used for reading, faces and detail. More: Macular Degeneration

Macular degeneration (AMD). Age-related wear of the macula that affects central vision; dry and wet forms. More: Macular Degeneration

Macular edema. Swelling of the macula from leaking fluid, which blurs central vision. It occurs with diabetes, vein blockages, inflammation and after some surgery.

Macular hole. A small break through the center of the retina. More: Macular Hole

Meibomian glands. The oil glands along the eyelid edges whose oil keeps tears from evaporating. More: Blepharitis and the Eyelid Glands

MIGS. Minimally invasive glaucoma surgery: small procedures, often done with cataract surgery, that improve the eye's own drain. More: MIGS

Monofocal lens. The standard lens implant, clear at one distance. More: Choosing a Lens Implant

Monovision. One eye set for distance and the other for near. More: Choosing a Lens Implant

Multifocal lens. A lens implant that splits light between distances to reduce the need for glasses. More: Choosing a Lens Implant

N

Nearsightedness (myopia). An eye that focuses light in front of the retina, so distance is blurred and near is clear. More: Your Glasses Prescription

Normal-tension glaucoma. Glaucoma damage with eye pressure in the normal range. More: Other Types of Glaucoma

O

OCT (optical coherence tomography). A quick, painless scan that photographs the layers of the retina and optic nerve in cross-section. More: Glaucoma Testing

Ocular hypertension. Eye pressure above the usual range with a healthy optic nerve. More: Glaucoma Suspect and Ocular Hypertension

OD, OS, OU. Right eye, left eye, both eyes, on prescriptions and drop instructions.

Optic nerve. The cable that carries what the eye sees to the brain; the part glaucoma damages. More: What Is Glaucoma?

P

Pachymetry. A quick measurement of the thickness of the cornea, which affects how pressure readings are interpreted. More: Glaucoma Testing

Posterior vitreous detachment (PVD). The gel inside the eye pulling away from the retina with age, which brings new floaters and flashes. More: Flashes and Floaters

Presbyopia. The age-related loss of near focus that makes reading glasses necessary, usually from the 40s. More: Your Glasses Prescription

Prostaglandin. The family of once-daily glaucoma drops, such as latanoprost, that lower pressure the most of any single drop. More: Glaucoma Eye Drops

Pterygium. A fleshy growth from the white of the eye onto the cornea, from sun and wind. More: Pterygium and Pinguecula

Punctal plug. A tiny plug in the tear drain opening that keeps your own tears on the eye longer. More: Treating Dry Eye

Pupil. The opening in the center of the iris that lets light in.

R

Retina. The light-sensing layer lining the back of the eye. More: Retina

Retinal detachment. The retina coming away from the wall of the eye, usually after a tear. It is an emergency. More: Retinal Tears and Detachment

Retinal vein occlusion. A blocked vein in the retina, causing bleeding and swelling. More: Retinal Vein Occlusion

S

Schlemm's canal. The channel behind the trabecular meshwork that carries fluid out of the eye. More: MIGS

Sclera. The white, tough outer wall of the eye.

Scleral lens. A large rigid contact lens that vaults over the cornea and rests on the white of the eye. More: Keratoconus

SLT (selective laser trabeculoplasty). An office laser that lowers eye pressure by treating the drain. More: SLT Laser

Steroid responder. Someone whose eye pressure rises with steroid drops or other steroids. More: Other Types of Glaucoma

T

Target pressure. The eye pressure your doctor aims for, set for each eye from the state of the optic nerve. More: Glaucoma Suspect and Ocular Hypertension

Toric lens. A lens implant or contact lens that corrects astigmatism. More: Choosing a Lens Implant

Trabecular meshwork. The eye's main drain, a fine filter in the drainage angle. More: How Treatments Lower Eye Pressure

Trabeculectomy. Glaucoma surgery that makes a new drain under the upper eyelid. More: Trabeculectomy

Tube shunt. A small tube and plate that carry fluid out of the eye, for glaucoma that needs surgery. More: Tube Shunt Surgery

U

Uveitis. Inflammation inside the eye. It can cause pain, redness, light sensitivity and blurred vision, and can raise eye pressure.

V

Visual acuity. How sharp your vision is on the letter chart; 20/20 is normal. 20/40 means you see at 20 feet what a typical eye sees at 40.

Visual field. How much you see around you while looking straight ahead, measured with a test in which you press a button when you see a light. More: Glaucoma Testing

Vitrectomy. Surgery that removes the gel inside the eye to repair the retina. More: Vitrectomy

Vitreous. The clear gel that fills the inside of the eye. More: Flashes and Floaters

Y

YAG capsulotomy. An office laser that clears a cloudy capsule behind a lens implant after cataract surgery. More: YAG Capsulotomy
