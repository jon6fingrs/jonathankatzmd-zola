Patient information

Trabeculectomy

Glaucoma surgery that makes a new drain under the upper eyelid, for pressure that drops and laser cannot control.

Scan for the full page, with an animation

What it is

The surgeon makes a small trapdoor in the wall of the eye under the upper lid. Fluid leaves through it and collects in a small blister, the bleb, hidden under the lid, where it is absorbed. Stitches in the trapdoor can be loosened in the weeks afterward to fine-tune the pressure, so the first months involve close follow-up.

The day of surgery

  • Outpatient, with a numbing block and sedation; about 45 to 60 minutes.
  • You go home with a patch and shield. Bring a driver.
  • Ask well beforehand about any blood thinners.

Recovery

  • Vision is often blurry for several weeks.
  • Drops: a steroid often eight times a day at first, tapered slowly over about twelve weeks; an antibiotic for the first week; an antibiotic ointment at bedtime for about two weeks. Glaucoma drops in the operated eye are usually stopped; keep using drops in the other eye unless told otherwise.
  • Visits: the next day, then weekly for several weeks. Stitch adjustments take a minute in the office.
  • Wear the shield at night for two weeks.
  • For about a month: no rubbing, no bending with your head below your waist, no lifting more than about ten pounds, no swimming or straining.

Call the same day if

  • Pain getting worse rather than better
  • Increasing redness, or discharge
  • A sudden drop in vision
  • Light sensitivity that starts after the first few days

For life: an eye that turns red, painful or sticky at any time, even years later, should be seen promptly.
