The words below come up at eye visits and on the pages of this site. Each has a short, plain meaning, and most link to the page that explains more. The [search](@/search.md) finds any of them across the whole site.

## A {#a}

**Accommodation.** The eye changing focus from far to near, by changing the shape of its lens. It fades with age (see presbyopia). More: [Your Glasses Prescription](@/learn/glasses-prescription.md)

**Amsler grid.** A square of straight lines used at home to check each eye for new distortion or a missing patch, an early sign of change in the macula. More: [Macular Degeneration](@/learn/macular-degeneration.md)

**Angle (drainage angle).** The corner inside the eye where the iris meets the cornea. The eye's drain, the trabecular meshwork, sits here. More: [Narrow Angles and Angle Closure](@/learn/angle-closure.md)

**Angle closure.** The iris blocking the drainage angle, which can raise eye pressure slowly or suddenly. More: [Narrow Angles and Angle Closure](@/learn/angle-closure.md)

**Anti-VEGF.** Medicines injected into the eye that stop abnormal blood vessels from leaking and growing, used for wet macular degeneration, diabetic eye disease and vein blockages. More: [Injections into the Eye](@/learn/eye-injections.md)

**Aqueous humor.** The clear fluid that fills the front of the eye. It is made all the time and drains away, and the balance between the two sets the eye pressure. More: [How Treatments Lower Eye Pressure](@/learn/eye-pressure.md)

**Astigmatism.** A front of the eye curved more like a football than a basketball, so the image is smeared in one direction. Glasses, contact lenses or a toric lens implant correct it. More: [Your Glasses Prescription](@/learn/glasses-prescription.md)

## B {#b}

**Beta blocker.** A family of glaucoma drops, such as timolol, that make the eye produce less fluid. More: [Glaucoma Eye Drops](@/learn/glaucoma-drops.md)

**Blepharitis.** Inflammation of the eyelid edges, often from blocked oil glands; the most common cause of dry eye. More: [Blepharitis and the Eyelid Glands](@/learn/blepharitis.md)

**Bleb.** The small blister under the upper eyelid where fluid collects and is absorbed after a trabeculectomy. More: [Trabeculectomy](@/learn/trabeculectomy.md)

## C {#c}

**Capsule.** The thin, clear bag that holds the eye's lens, and later the lens implant. When it turns cloudy after cataract surgery, a laser clears it. More: [YAG Capsulotomy](@/learn/yag-capsulotomy.md)

**Cataract.** Clouding of the eye's natural lens, which blurs vision and causes glare. It is treated by replacing the lens. More: [Cataract Surgery](@/learn/cataract-surgery.md)

**Chalazion.** A firm, usually painless lump in the eyelid from a blocked oil gland. More: [Blepharitis and the Eyelid Glands](@/learn/blepharitis.md)

**Ciliary body.** The ring of tissue behind the iris that makes the eye's fluid, and holds the lens in place. More: [What Is Glaucoma?](@/learn/what-is-glaucoma.md)

**Conjunctiva.** The thin, clear skin over the white of the eye and the inside of the eyelids.

**Cornea.** The clear dome at the front of the eye. It lets light in and does most of the eye's focusing. More: [Cornea](@/learn/cornea/_index.md)

**Cross-linking.** A treatment that stiffens the cornea with vitamin B2 drops and ultraviolet light, to stop keratoconus getting worse. More: [Keratoconus](@/learn/keratoconus.md)

**Cup-to-disc ratio.** How much of the optic nerve's surface is taken up by its central cup. A cup that is large or growing can be a sign of glaucoma. More: [Glaucoma Testing](@/learn/glaucoma-testing.md)

**Cyclophotocoagulation (CPC).** A laser applied through the white of the eye that lowers pressure by reducing how much fluid the eye makes. More: [Micropulse and CPC Laser](@/learn/cyclophotocoagulation.md)

## D {#d}

**Dilation.** Widening the pupil with drops so the doctor can see the lens, retina and optic nerve. Vision is blurry and bright light uncomfortable for a few hours.

**Diopter.** The unit of focusing power used in glasses prescriptions; minus for nearsighted, plus for farsighted. More: [Your Glasses Prescription](@/learn/glasses-prescription.md)

**DMEK and DSAEK.** Partial-thickness corneal transplants that replace only the cornea's inner pump layer, mainly for Fuchs dystrophy. More: [Corneal Transplants](@/learn/corneal-transplant.md)

**Drusen.** Small yellow deposits under the retina, the first sign of macular degeneration. More: [Macular Degeneration](@/learn/macular-degeneration.md)

**Dry eye.** A problem with the tear film, from too few tears or tears that evaporate too fast. More: [What Is Dry Eye?](@/learn/what-is-dry-eye.md)

## E {#e}

**Endothelium.** The single layer of pump cells lining the inside of the cornea, which keeps it clear. More: [Fuchs Dystrophy](@/learn/fuchs-dystrophy.md)

**Epiretinal membrane.** A thin film on the surface of the macula that can wrinkle it and distort vision; also called macular pucker. More: [Epiretinal Membrane](@/learn/epiretinal-membrane.md)

**Extended depth of focus (EDOF) lens.** A lens implant that gives clear vision from distance to arm's length. More: [Choosing a Lens Implant](@/learn/lens-implants.md)

## F {#f}

**Farsightedness (hyperopia).** An eye that focuses light behind the retina, so near vision blurs first. More: [Your Glasses Prescription](@/learn/glasses-prescription.md)

**Floaters.** Specks, threads or cobwebs that drift across vision, from clumps in the gel inside the eye. A sudden shower of new ones needs a same-day exam. More: [Flashes and Floaters](@/learn/flashes-and-floaters.md)

**Fovea.** The tiny pit at the center of the macula that gives the sharpest vision.

**Fuchs dystrophy.** An inherited loss of the cornea's pump cells that makes it swell and turn hazy, often worst in the morning. More: [Fuchs Dystrophy](@/learn/fuchs-dystrophy.md)

**Fundus.** The back of the eye as the doctor sees it: the retina, the macula, the optic nerve and the blood vessels.

## G {#g}

**Glaucoma.** Damage to the optic nerve, usually related to eye pressure, that takes side vision first and without symptoms. More: [What Is Glaucoma?](@/learn/what-is-glaucoma.md)

**Glaucoma suspect.** Someone with a finding that raises the question of glaucoma, such as high pressure or a suspicious nerve, without proof of damage. More: [Glaucoma Suspect and Ocular Hypertension](@/learn/glaucoma-suspect.md)

**Gonioscopy.** An exam with a mirrored contact lens that lets the doctor see the drainage angle. More: [Glaucoma Testing](@/learn/glaucoma-testing.md)

## H {#h}

**Halos.** Rings or glow around lights, especially at night. They can come from a cataract, a multifocal lens implant, a swollen cornea, or, with pain and redness, an attack of angle closure.

**Hyphema.** Blood in the front of the eye, after an injury or some eye surgery. It usually clears within days.

## I {#i}

**Intraocular lens (IOL).** The clear artificial lens placed in the eye during cataract surgery. More: [Choosing a Lens Implant](@/learn/lens-implants.md)

**Intraocular pressure (IOP).** The pressure inside the eye, measured in millimeters of mercury (mm Hg). More: [How Treatments Lower Eye Pressure](@/learn/eye-pressure.md)

**Iridotomy.** A tiny laser opening in the iris that opens a narrow drainage angle. More: [Laser Iridotomy](@/learn/laser-iridotomy.md)

**Iris.** The colored part of the eye. Its opening is the pupil.

## K {#k}

**Keratitis.** Inflammation or infection of the cornea. More: [Corneal Infections](@/learn/corneal-infections.md)

**Keratoconus.** A cornea that thins and bulges into a cone, blurring and distorting vision. More: [Keratoconus](@/learn/keratoconus.md)

## L {#l}

**Lens.** The clear structure behind the iris that fine-tunes the eye's focus. When it clouds, it is a cataract.

## M {#m}

**Macula.** The small central part of the retina used for reading, faces and detail. More: [Macular Degeneration](@/learn/macular-degeneration.md)

**Macular degeneration (AMD).** Age-related wear of the macula that affects central vision; dry and wet forms. More: [Macular Degeneration](@/learn/macular-degeneration.md)

**Macular edema.** Swelling of the macula from leaking fluid, which blurs central vision. It occurs with diabetes, vein blockages, inflammation and after some surgery.

**Macular hole.** A small break through the center of the retina. More: [Macular Hole](@/learn/macular-hole.md)

**Meibomian glands.** The oil glands along the eyelid edges whose oil keeps tears from evaporating. More: [Blepharitis and the Eyelid Glands](@/learn/blepharitis.md)

**MIGS.** Minimally invasive glaucoma surgery: small procedures, often done with cataract surgery, that improve the eye's own drain. More: [MIGS](@/learn/migs.md)

**Monofocal lens.** The standard lens implant, clear at one distance. More: [Choosing a Lens Implant](@/learn/lens-implants.md)

**Monovision.** One eye set for distance and the other for near. More: [Choosing a Lens Implant](@/learn/lens-implants.md)

**Multifocal lens.** A lens implant that splits light between distances to reduce the need for glasses. More: [Choosing a Lens Implant](@/learn/lens-implants.md)

## N {#n}

**Nearsightedness (myopia).** An eye that focuses light in front of the retina, so distance is blurred and near is clear. More: [Your Glasses Prescription](@/learn/glasses-prescription.md)

**Normal-tension glaucoma.** Glaucoma damage with eye pressure in the normal range. More: [Other Types of Glaucoma](@/learn/other-glaucoma-types.md)

## O {#o}

**OCT (optical coherence tomography).** A quick, painless scan that photographs the layers of the retina and optic nerve in cross-section. More: [Glaucoma Testing](@/learn/glaucoma-testing.md)

**Ocular hypertension.** Eye pressure above the usual range with a healthy optic nerve. More: [Glaucoma Suspect and Ocular Hypertension](@/learn/glaucoma-suspect.md)

**OD, OS, OU.** Right eye, left eye, both eyes, on prescriptions and drop instructions.

**Optic nerve.** The cable that carries what the eye sees to the brain; the part glaucoma damages. More: [What Is Glaucoma?](@/learn/what-is-glaucoma.md)

## P {#p}

**Pachymetry.** A quick measurement of the thickness of the cornea, which affects how pressure readings are interpreted. More: [Glaucoma Testing](@/learn/glaucoma-testing.md)

**Posterior vitreous detachment (PVD).** The gel inside the eye pulling away from the retina with age, which brings new floaters and flashes. More: [Flashes and Floaters](@/learn/flashes-and-floaters.md)

**Presbyopia.** The age-related loss of near focus that makes reading glasses necessary, usually from the 40s. More: [Your Glasses Prescription](@/learn/glasses-prescription.md)

**Prostaglandin.** The family of once-daily glaucoma drops, such as latanoprost, that lower pressure the most of any single drop. More: [Glaucoma Eye Drops](@/learn/glaucoma-drops.md)

**Pterygium.** A fleshy growth from the white of the eye onto the cornea, from sun and wind. More: [Pterygium and Pinguecula](@/learn/pterygium.md)

**Punctal plug.** A tiny plug in the tear drain opening that keeps your own tears on the eye longer. More: [Treating Dry Eye](@/learn/dry-eye-treatment.md)

**Pupil.** The opening in the center of the iris that lets light in.

## R {#r}

**Retina.** The light-sensing layer lining the back of the eye. More: [Retina](@/learn/retina/_index.md)

**Retinal detachment.** The retina coming away from the wall of the eye, usually after a tear. It is an emergency. More: [Retinal Tears and Detachment](@/learn/retinal-detachment.md)

**Retinal vein occlusion.** A blocked vein in the retina, causing bleeding and swelling. More: [Retinal Vein Occlusion](@/learn/retinal-vein-occlusion.md)

## S {#s}

**Schlemm's canal.** The channel behind the trabecular meshwork that carries fluid out of the eye. More: [MIGS](@/learn/migs.md)

**Sclera.** The white, tough outer wall of the eye.

**Scleral lens.** A large rigid contact lens that vaults over the cornea and rests on the white of the eye. More: [Keratoconus](@/learn/keratoconus.md)

**SLT (selective laser trabeculoplasty).** An office laser that lowers eye pressure by treating the drain. More: [SLT Laser](@/learn/slt-laser.md)

**Steroid responder.** Someone whose eye pressure rises with steroid drops or other steroids. More: [Other Types of Glaucoma](@/learn/other-glaucoma-types.md)

## T {#t}

**Target pressure.** The eye pressure your doctor aims for, set for each eye from the state of the optic nerve. More: [Glaucoma Suspect and Ocular Hypertension](@/learn/glaucoma-suspect.md)

**Toric lens.** A lens implant or contact lens that corrects astigmatism. More: [Choosing a Lens Implant](@/learn/lens-implants.md)

**Trabecular meshwork.** The eye's main drain, a fine filter in the drainage angle. More: [How Treatments Lower Eye Pressure](@/learn/eye-pressure.md)

**Trabeculectomy.** Glaucoma surgery that makes a new drain under the upper eyelid. More: [Trabeculectomy](@/learn/trabeculectomy.md)

**Tube shunt.** A small tube and plate that carry fluid out of the eye, for glaucoma that needs surgery. More: [Tube Shunt Surgery](@/learn/tube-shunt-surgery.md)

## U {#u}

**Uveitis.** Inflammation inside the eye. It can cause pain, redness, light sensitivity and blurred vision, and can raise eye pressure.

## V {#v}

**Visual acuity.** How sharp your vision is on the letter chart; 20/20 is normal. 20/40 means you see at 20 feet what a typical eye sees at 40.

**Visual field.** How much you see around you while looking straight ahead, measured with a test in which you press a button when you see a light. More: [Glaucoma Testing](@/learn/glaucoma-testing.md)

**Vitrectomy.** Surgery that removes the gel inside the eye to repair the retina. More: [Vitrectomy](@/learn/vitrectomy.md)

**Vitreous.** The clear gel that fills the inside of the eye. More: [Flashes and Floaters](@/learn/flashes-and-floaters.md)

## Y {#y}

**YAG capsulotomy.** An office laser that clears a cloudy capsule behind a lens implant after cataract surgery. More: [YAG Capsulotomy](@/learn/yag-capsulotomy.md)
