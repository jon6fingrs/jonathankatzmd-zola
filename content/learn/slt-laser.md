**Selective laser trabeculoplasty (SLT)** is an office procedure that lowers eye pressure by treating the trabecular meshwork, the eye's natural drain. It takes a few minutes in the office and has no cuts or stitches. It is used for open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension, either as a first treatment instead of drops or alongside them.

There are two ways to deliver it, and the animation shows both: **standard SLT**, aimed through a contact lens, and **direct SLT (DSLT)**, which uses no lens. Press **Play** to watch, or tap a step to go through it at your own pace.

## How it works

The laser delivers very short pulses of light that are absorbed by pigment in the drainage meshwork. The energy is low and selective, so it does not burn or scar the tissue. Instead it triggers a biological response that, over several weeks, improves how well the meshwork drains fluid. A large clinical trial (the LiGHT trial) found that SLT as a first treatment controlled pressure as well as drops in most patients, with many needing no drops at all for years afterward.

## Standard SLT and direct SLT

Both use the same kind of laser energy on the same tissue, and both aim for the same result. They differ in how the light gets to the drain.

- **Standard SLT.** The drain is hidden behind the edge of the cornea, so a contact lens with a mirror is placed on the numbed eye. The doctor looks through it and places the laser spots one at a time around the drain.
- **Direct SLT (DSLT).** The laser is sent straight through the edge of the cornea (the limbus) to the drain underneath, so no contact lens is needed and nothing touches the eye. The machine's camera finds the edge of the cornea and follows the eye, then delivers about 120 pulses in a ring in two to three seconds.

Direct SLT is newer and is available at some offices. Your doctor will tell you which is planned for you and why; either may suit you better depending on your eyes and where you are seen.

## Why your doctor might suggest it

- To avoid or delay starting daily drops
- To reduce the number of drops you use
- When drops are hard to use, cause side effects, or are not being taken reliably
- When pressure is not quite at target on current treatment

SLT lowers pressure by roughly 20 to 30 percent on average, about the same as a single drop. It works in most but not all eyes, and the effect can wear off over one to five years; it can usually be repeated.

## The visit

1. A pressure-lowering drop and a numbing drop are placed in the eye.
2. You sit at the laser with your chin and forehead on the rests.
3. **Standard SLT:** a contact lens is placed on the eye to hold the lids open and focus the laser on the drain. You may feel light pressure from the lens. The doctor applies the laser around the drain, usually 50 to 100 spots, over a few minutes. You will see flashes of light and may feel brief tingling or a small pinch with some pulses.
4. **Direct SLT:** there is no contact lens. You look at a target light while the machine lines up, and the treatment itself lasts two to three seconds. You will see a brief flicker of light.
5. Pressure is checked again before you leave, or at a visit soon after.

Both eyes can be treated the same day or on separate visits. You can drive afterward if your vision is adequate and you were not dilated, but many people prefer to bring a driver.

## After the laser

- **Mild ache or light sensitivity** for a day or two is common. Over-the-counter pain relievers and sunglasses help.
- **Blurred vision** from the gel used with the contact lens (standard SLT) clears within a few hours.
- **Redness or small red spots on the white of the eye** can appear after direct SLT, where the laser passed through. They are harmless and fade in a few days.
- You may be given an anti-inflammatory drop for a few days, or none at all, depending on your doctor's preference.
- **Keep using your glaucoma drops** unless you are told to stop. SLT takes four to six weeks to reach its full effect, so drops are not usually changed until a follow-up pressure check.
- There are no activity restrictions.

## Call the same day if

- Eye pain that is getting worse rather than better
- A marked drop in vision
- Increasing redness

See [When to Call the Same Day](@/learn/when-to-call.md) for other urgent symptoms.

## Risks

SLT is one of the safest glaucoma procedures. A temporary rise in eye pressure in the first hours is the main concern, which is why pressure is checked afterward and a preventive drop is used. Inflammation is mild and brief. Rarely, pressure rises and stays higher, or the laser does not lower pressure enough and other treatment is needed. The laser does not affect your vision, prescription or ability to have cataract or other surgery later.
