Most eye problems can wait for a scheduled visit. The ones on this page should not. A few need an emergency room right away; they are listed first. For the rest, **contact your eye doctor the same day** and describe what is happening, so you can be seen quickly. Many eye practices have an after-hours number or a doctor on call. If you cannot reach an eye doctor and the problem is severe, go to an emergency room.

This page is general information, not a diagnosis or a substitute for advice from a doctor who knows your eyes.

## Go to an emergency room now

Call 911 (or your local emergency number), or go straight to an emergency room, without waiting to reach an eye doctor, for:

- **Sudden loss of vision in one eye**, or in part of one eye's vision, even if it does not hurt and even if it comes back within minutes. A blocked artery to the eye is a kind of stroke, and treatment works best when it starts within hours.
- **Sudden double vision**
- **Any sudden change in vision with weakness, numbness, a drooping face, trouble speaking or a severe headache.** These can be signs of a stroke.

## Sudden changes in vision

- Sudden blurring of vision in one eye
- A curtain or shadow that spreads in from the edge of your vision
- Straight lines that suddenly look bent or wavy in one eye

## Flashes and floaters

- A sudden shower of new floaters
- Flashes of light, like a camera flash at the edge of your vision
- Either of these followed by a shadow or curtain

A few long-standing floaters are normal. A sudden change can mean a tear in the retina, which is treatable when caught early. See [Flashes and Floaters](@/learn/flashes-and-floaters.md).

## A painful or red eye

- Eye pain with nausea, halos around lights and a red eye. This can be an attack of [angle-closure glaucoma](@/learn/laser-iridotomy.md).
- Severe eye pain, with or without redness
- A red, painful eye with light sensitivity or reduced vision, especially if you wear contact lenses

## After surgery or laser

Some aching, redness and blur are expected at first, and each procedure page describes what is normal. Call the same day if you have:

- Pain that is getting worse rather than better
- Vision that gets worse after it had started to improve
- Increasing redness, or discharge from the eye
- New flashes, a shower of floaters, or a curtain over part of your vision
- Light sensitivity that develops after the first few days

## Injuries

- **A chemical splashed in the eye:** rinse with clean water right away for at least 15 minutes, then get emergency care. Do not wait to call first.
- **Something has cut or pierced the eye:** do not press on it or try to remove anything. Cover it loosely and go to an emergency room.
- A hard blow to the eye with pain, reduced vision or blood visible in the eye

## Not sure?

If something about your eye or your vision has changed suddenly and you are worried, call. It is always fine to ask.
