The artificial tears shelf at a pharmacy is long and confusing. The differences that matter are few: whether the drop has a preservative, how thick it is, and when you use it. Answer the questions below to see what to look for.

## The kinds, in plain terms

- **Standard artificial tears** come in an ordinary bottle with a preservative that keeps the bottle sterile. They are fine up to about four times a day.
- **Preservative-free tears** come in single-use vials or in bottles with a special tip. Choose them if you need tears more than four times a day, wear contact lenses, have sensitive eyes, or already use other eye drops every day. The preservative itself can irritate the surface with frequent use.
- **Gel drops** are thicker. They last longer and blur vision for a minute or two, so they suit bad days and evenings.
- **Ointment** is the thickest. It is for bedtime, because it blurs vision until it is blinked away.
- **Tears with oil** in them, often labelled "lipid" or "for evaporative dry eye", help when the oil glands in the eyelids are blocked.

## Getting the most from them

- **Use them on a schedule.** Three or four times a day, every day, works better than waiting until the eyes hurt.
- **One drop is enough.** The eye cannot hold more.
- **Leave five minutes** between different drops, and put tears in last if you also use prescription drops, unless you are told otherwise. Ointment always goes in last.
- **Single-use vials** are meant to be thrown away after use. Do not keep an open vial for the next day.
- **Do not let the tip touch** the eye, lids or fingers.
- **Avoid "get the red out" drops.** They shrink blood vessels and do not treat dryness, and the redness often comes back worse.

Technique is on [Using Eye Drops](@/learn/using-eye-drops.md), and all of these are pictured in the [Eye Medication Reference](@/patients/eye-drops.md).

## When tears are not enough

If you need tears more than six times a day, or they have not helped after a few weeks, it is time to be seen. [Treating Dry Eye](@/learn/dry-eye-treatment.md) covers the next steps, including lid care, prescription drops and punctal plugs.
