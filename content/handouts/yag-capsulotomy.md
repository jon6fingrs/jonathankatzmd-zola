## Why it is done

Months or years after cataract surgery, the thin capsule behind the lens implant can turn hazy. This is common and is not a complication. The laser makes a clear opening in its center, which usually brings vision back to where it was after the surgery. It is normally needed only once in each eye.

## The visit

- Drops dilate the pupil. Some doctors add a drop to prevent a rise in eye pressure.
- You sit at the laser with your chin on a rest. You see flashes and hear soft clicks. It does not hurt and takes a minute or two.
- Usually nothing touches the eye.
- Bring a driver: the dilating drops blur vision for several hours.

</div><div class="jk-handout__col">

## Afterward

- Vision is blurry for a few hours, then clears. Most people notice the improvement by the next day.
- New floaters are expected. They are small pieces of the capsule and fade over a few weeks.
- You may be given an anti-inflammatory drop for a few days, or none.
- Keep using any glaucoma drops as usual.
- There are no activity restrictions.

<div class="jk-handout__call">
<h2>Call the same day if</h2>
<ul>
<li>Flashes of light, or a sudden shower of new floaters</li>
<li>A curtain or shadow in your side vision</li>
<li>Eye pain</li>
<li>Vision that gets worse instead of better</li>
</ul>
