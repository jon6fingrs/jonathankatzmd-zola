## What it is

A soft silicone tube is placed just inside the front of the eye and carries fluid to a thin plate on the wall of the eye, under the upper lid, where it is absorbed. The tube is covered with a patch. Some devices have a valve and work right away (Ahmed). Others have no valve and are tied off for the first four to six weeks while healing takes place (Baerveldt and similar); drops control the pressure until the tube opens.

## The day of surgery

- Outpatient, with a numbing block and sedation; about an hour.
- You go home with a patch and shield. Bring a driver.

## Recovery

- **Drops:** a steroid often at first, tapered over about six weeks; an antibiotic for the first week; an antibiotic ointment at bedtime for about two weeks. With a non-valved device, keep using the glaucoma drops you are told to until the tube opens.
- **Visits:** the next day, one week, then every one to two weeks for the first six weeks.
- Wear the shield at night for two weeks. For the first month, avoid rubbing the eye, swimming, heavy lifting and bending with your head down.
- **Normal at first:** blurry vision, a scratchy feeling, mild ache, and a red, swollen eye for one to two weeks. The eye may feel slightly full where the plate sits. Early double vision usually settles.

<div class="jk-handout__call">
<h2>Call the same day if</h2>
<ul>
<li>Pain increases rather than eases</li>
<li>Vision suddenly worsens</li>
<li>The eye becomes much redder or has discharge</li>
<li>You can see or feel the tube, or the eye feels very hard or very soft</li>
</ul>
